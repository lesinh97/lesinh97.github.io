Armagnac comes from Gascony in southwest France and predates Cognac by about 150 years, with distilling recorded there from the early 1300s. It is distilled once in a continuous alambic rather than twice in a pot, which leaves more of the grape in the spirit, and then aged long in local oak.

Château Garreau sits at Labastide-d'Armagnac in the Landes, in the part of Bas-Armagnac growers call the golden triangle. The estate runs to 82 hectares and keeps the Écomusée de l'Armagnac on site, a stills collection and a growers' museum in a 17th century barn. It grows the four Armagnac grapes, and this bottling is Baco, the hybrid that is native to the region and gives Bas-Armagnac its weight.

Distilled in 2000 and bottled at 23 years, at 49.7 percent, with no chill filtration and no added colour. Twenty three years is where the method pays off. The wood has taken the edges off without flattening the fruit, and the rancio that only comes with age is the first thing in the glass.
