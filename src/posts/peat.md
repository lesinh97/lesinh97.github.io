---
title: "Peat is a texture, not a flavour"
date: "2026-08-30"
summary: "After a year of notes, the thing I keep getting wrong about smoky whisky."
topics: ["Whisky", "Tasting"]
---

I have been scoring whisky in a spreadsheet, and now a ledger, for long enough
to see my own bad habits. The loudest one: I score peat as if it were a flavour
sitting alongside fruit and oak, when it behaves much more like a texture.

## What the notes show

Look at the bottles I rated highest and the peat column is not what separates
them. What separates them is whether the smoke arrives early or late. Early
smoke flattens everything behind it. Late smoke reads as depth.

That distinction does not fit in a zero-to-five number, which is a limitation of
my own scoring, not of the whisky.

## What I am changing

Nothing, yet. The point of writing notes down is that you get to notice the
pattern first and decide what to do about it later. For now I am simply adding
a line to the finish note saying when the smoke shows up.

Ask me in another fifty bottles.
